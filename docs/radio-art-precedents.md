# Who is listening? Radio art precedents for RDS Poet

The closest precedent I found is Esteban Agosín’s *M(ol)AR, Hybrid Landscape*. In 2024 he used radio reception, machine listening and GPT-2 to make a place speak. A related work of his reached home FM receivers. The distinction matters for RDS Poet. In one piece listeners stood among speakers on a beach. In the other they could tune in from home. Your project gives that distant listener another part to play. They can hear a note change and look down to see what the station just wrote. [Agosín’s ISEA paper](https://www.isea-symposium-archives.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_Otero_M-ol-AR-HYBRID-LANDSCAPE-Radio-waves-as-an-extended-ecology.pdf) · [Wave Farm broadcast archive](https://wavefarm.org/radio/wgxc/schedule/qbvpnb)

## From an antenna to a sentence

**Esteban Agosín, *M(ol)AR, Hybrid Landscape* (Seattle, April 2024).** On South Beach at Discovery Park, seawater antennas fed software-defined radio receivers. A sound classifier assigned familiar labels to unfamiliar electromagnetic sounds. A label could call up a field recording from Radio Aporee. Agosín also trained GPT-2 on two books by Timothy Morton to write poetic descriptions around frequently recognized categories. A synthesized voice occasionally entered the mix. Fourteen speakers carried the work to people at the site. Agosín documents real-time classification and the GPT-2 writing process. The account leaves open whether each utterance was generated anew during the installation. What RDS Poet can borrow is the decision to let an error survive. If a receiver’s hiss suggests rain, the poem can tell us what sort of rain the machine imagines. [Artist’s installation account](https://estebanagosin.cl/molar.html) · [ISEA 2025 paper](https://www.isea-symposium-archives.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_Otero_M-ol-AR-HYBRID-LANDSCAPE-Radio-waves-as-an-extended-ecology.pdf)

**Agosín, *Imaginary Radio* (2022, broadcast on WGXC in 2024).** A loop antenna and SDR supplied material for a series of improvisations. A classifier’s guesses became search terms for Freesound, bringing recordings of distant places into contact with local signals. Wave Farm aired a compilation on WGXC 90.7 FM on December 28, 2024. The station documents sound recognition, archive recordings and their descriptions. GPT-2 is documented for *M(ol)AR*. These are two separate works. Heard together, they pose a question for the poet’s transmitter. How much of the place where the antenna is standing can someone else hear from home? [Artist’s radio works](https://www.estebanagosin.cl/eng/radio.html) · [WGXC program and artist’s description](https://wavefarm.org/radio/wgxc/schedule/qbvpnb)

## From a sentence to a radio audience

**Machine Listening, *After Words* (2022, broadcast in 2023).** Sean Dockray, James Parker and Joel Stern assembled scenes from machine-learning audio datasets. They wrote the script with an autoregressive language model and had people perform it. Radiophrenia programmed the work on August 25, 2023, in its Glasgow 87.9 FM broadcast. This gives a documented example of language-model writing reaching terrestrial radio listeners. The work exposes a wrinkle in machine listening. A voice that sounds spontaneously sad might belong to an actor who was asked to perform sadness for a dataset. In RDS Poet, the listener could similarly hear the distance between the actual sound and the line the model assigns to it. [Exhibition notes and audio](https://data-relations.netlify.app/) · [Radiophrenia schedule](https://2023.radiophrenia.scot/schedule/) · [Festival frequency](https://2023.radiophrenia.scot/)

**Joey Orr and Chris Campbell, *Midnight Bill* (WREK Atlanta, 2011).** This is the most relevant precedent for the iPod itself. A listener tuning to 91.1 FM could hear the program and read extra narrative details, names and dates on the receiver. Campbell’s software sent scripted strings at timecode markers. The display could tell you something the soundtrack left out. For a generated poem, a useful equivalent would be to let the sound establish a regular ticking while the display quietly identifies what is being counted. [Orr’s project credit](https://joeyorr.com/cv/) · [Campbell’s account of the RDS system](https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/wrek-beefs-up-rds-hd-text-display-for-special-program)

## Two performance references

**Tetsuo Kogawa, *SDR Jockey* (Wave Farm commission, broadcast by Radio Panik in 2025).** Kogawa composed with the act of tuning SDRs and incorporated signals from multiple transmitters. The broadcaster provides the piece to hear. Listen for what happens when a tuning gesture lasts longer than the sound it first uncovers. That suggests a musical role for waiting between generated lines. [Radio Panik program and recording](https://www.radiopanik.org/emissions/radia/sdr-jockey-by-tetsuo-kogawa-for-wave-farm/)

**Sven Hollowell, Pete Bennett and Paul Marshall, *Multi-Agent Swarm Syntax* (NIME 2026).** Their system lets performers invite LLMs to edit Strudel alongside them, with each agent’s typing visible at its own cursor. The audience can see composition happening. RDS Poet has a much smaller public surface. A held note and a lingering line could let someone hear the time the model takes to answer, even with the laptop closed. [Authors’ conference paper](https://nime.org/proc/nime2026_171/index.html)

## What to try on 87.5

Give the receiver a repeated sound to listen for. Let its brightness determine the register of the next Strudel phrase. Let the poem carry one object across several lines while that sound changes. A listener joining midway gets something to recognize. For example, a dry opening tick might accompany “one spoon in the drawer.” A brighter, doubled tick could bring “someone has set a second place.” If the input falls quiet, leave the second place set for another cycle. Those are proposed lines for this project.

This review traces artist accounts, station schedules, exhibition records and conference proceedings. I found evidence for SDR-to-language, language-model writing broadcast on FM, and timed literary text on RDS. I did not find a documented artwork in those sources in which live SDR reception conditions a language model that sends both fresh writing and a new musical pattern back over terrestrial FM/RDS. That is a useful description of this particular work and a limited search result, not a claim to priority.
